# Do the ads say "AI"? Banner-by-banner review

**Short answer:** You're right. 11 of the 20 banners don't say what you do. The fix is **not** to add "AI" to every hook. Make one change to the template and rewrite about 12 sublines:

1. **Change the audience chip from "למשרדי רואי חשבון" to "AI למשרדי רואי חשבון".** It's the first thing a Hebrew reader sees (top right), it's already on every banner, it adds no new line, and it matches the Meta Title.
2. **Make every subline say who does the work.** Use "AI ש…" or "אנחנו בונים…", never a bare "המערכת" ("the system"). Today the failing sublines read like ads for a software product, not for a partner who builds inside your systems.

## 2. Recommendation: explicit AI, but in the right place

**Yes, every banner should carry an explicit category signal. Put it in the chip, not the hook.**

- **Why the chip.** In RTL the eye lands top right first. "AI למשרדי רואי חשבון" answers *who* and *what* in one glance, before the hook. It adds no line, so text density stays the same. The renderer notes that an earlier campaign review rejected a logo tagline for legibility, and the chip gets around that. It also matches the Meta Title and the Google H1, so the whole funnel tells one story.
- **Why not in every hook.** The hooks work because they're the reader's own pain in their own words. Starting them with "AI" turns them into one more "AI tool for X" ad. Israeli SMB owners are seeing a flood of these right now, and my estimate (not measured) is that a pain-first hook beats an AI-first hook on scroll-stop. Use the pain to stop the scroll, then the chip and subline to qualify.
- **Self-qualification.** Saying "AI" up front filters out people who want a cheap bookkeeping app or a job. Fewer clicks, better calls. That's the right trade, because the goal is booked calls, not cheap traffic.
- **Meta text-in-image.** The old 20% rule is gone. Very text-heavy images may still get somewhat less delivery (an estimate, not a published threshold). The chip change adds about two characters, so the risk doesn't change.

**Wording:**
- **"טרנספורמציית AI": not on banners.** It's consultant-speak. To the owner of a distribution company it promises a big, expensive, abstract project, which is exactly what "no price" makes them fear. Keep it on the website and LinkedIn company page, where the reader is already evaluating you.
- **"אוטומציה ו-AI": good for Google search**, because owners search "אוטומציה" (it's already your keyword). On banners it's a little generic.
- **"AI שעובד בתוך המערכות שלכם"** (AI that works inside your systems): the most natural and credible wording, and it carries the difference. Use it in the Meta Title (concrete form: "AI בתוך התוכנה/ה-ERP שכבר יש לכם").
- **Chip:** "AI ל{audience}" (AI for {audience}). Short, plain, and the same as your current Title.

## 4. Template design changes (scripts/make-ad-images.mjs + ad-copy.mjs)

1. **Chip text:** `AI ${who}`, which renders as "AI למשרדי רואי חשבון", "AI לחברות הפצה ויבוא", "AI לסוכנויות ביטוח", "AI למשרדי עורכי דין", "AI ליזמים ולחברות בנייה". Wrap "AI" in an LTR span so it stays on the right of the Hebrew. On the wide (LinkedIn) size the chip is small; raise it from about 0.8× to about 0.9× scale.
2. **Footer gets a verb:** "קובעים שיחה של 30 דקות, בלי התחייבות ←" (book a 30-minute call). The left-pointing arrow means "forward" in RTL and points toward beenco.tech.
3. **Keep the logo clean.** No tagline under "bee & co.", in line with the earlier legibility review. The chip does the category work.
4. **New copy rule in the ad-copy.mjs header comment:** "Every subline names the actor, 'AI ש…' or 'אנחנו בונים…'. Never a bare 'המערכת'. Never promise a new system: always 'בתוך מה שכבר יש לכם'."
5. **No "טרנספורמציה" on any banner.** Keep it for the site and LinkedIn page copy.
6. **Minor:** insurance-chatgpt-portrait leaves "מותר?" alone on the last line. Force the break as "ChatGPT כבר במשרד. / מישהו החליט / מה מותר?".
